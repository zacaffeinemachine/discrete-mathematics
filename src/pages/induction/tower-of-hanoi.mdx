Three pegs $A$, $B$, $C$ stand on a board. On peg $A$ rests a tower of $n$ discs of
distinct sizes, in decreasing order of size, the largest at the bottom.

> A move takes the top disc of one peg and places it on top of another peg, provided it is
> not placed on a smaller disc. Show that the whole tower can be transferred from peg $A$
> to peg $C$, and count the moves this requires.

## What to notice

Try it with two discs, then three, then four, and watch what you do with your hands: you
clear the small discs out of the way, move the big one, and bring the small ones back.
That is the whole method, and it does not care how many "small ones" there are.

**Ask the expert** does the clearing-away in one go. It becomes available exactly when the
$n-1$ smaller discs are stacked together on a single peg — which is the only situation the
argument ever needs. Press it, move the largest disc yourself, and press it again.

The largest disc never gets in anyone's way while it sits at the bottom, because every
other disc is smaller and may legally rest on it. That one observation is what makes the
delegation honest.
