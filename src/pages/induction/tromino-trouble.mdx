Imagine a square grid of size $2^n \times 2^n$, perfectly uniform except for one flaw:
exactly one unit square has been removed, from an arbitrary location. An **L-tromino** is
an L-shaped tile made of three unit squares.

> For every $n \geq 1$, a $2^n \times 2^n$ grid with one unit square removed can be covered
> by L-trominoes with no overlaps and no gaps.

The hole is not a blemish on the problem; it is what makes the problem possible. A tile
covers three squares, so a board can only be covered when its square count is divisible by
three — and $4^n$ never is, while $4^n - 1$ always is.

## What to notice

Play first, on the $4 \times 4$ board. Then switch to **Delegate** and watch what the
quartering does.

Cut the board into four quadrants. The hole is in one of them. Now lay a single tile across
the three inner corners of the *other* three quadrants — and look at what you have: four
boards of half the side, each missing exactly one square. Each of them is the problem you
started with. Press **Quarter again** to do the same thing to all four at once, and again,
until there is nothing left to cut.

Move the hole before you start. The quartering never needed to know where it was.
